## Problems

### Potential Gap

One concept that resonates with my current self is the "Potential Gap." It is simply the gap between where you want to be and where you are right now. As simple as it sounds, it is something that many of us overlook. I noticed this when I read *The Miracle Morning* by Hal Elrod and an article titled "How to Fix Your Entire Life in 1 Day" by Dan Koe. In addition, I came across an Instagram reel that describes the same concept. Check out the [[#Appendix]] for more details!

As I understand it, this potential gap is actually a double-edged sword. On the one hand, we often focus on the negative side. On the other hand, it can motivate us to become the best version of ourselves—that is, to get where we want to be. Therefore, I must focus more on the positive aspects, though doing so is not as straightforward as it seems. Maybe the reason for this is exactly as Dan Koe describes:

- You aren't where you want to be because you aren't the person who would be there.
- You aren't where you want to be because you don't want to be there.
- You aren't where you want to be because you're afraid to be there.

He also described the approach to overcoming this problem:

- The life you want lies within a specific level of mind.
- Intelligence is the ability to get what you want out of life.
- Turn Your Life Into a Video Game.

I have always believed that every problem has its own solution. Thus, I believe that with God's help, I can overcome this challenge.

### Fear

I once watched a video of Jackie Chan and Muhammad Ali talking about fear. Jackie Chan said that he did not live according to the expectations of others, but according to his own. Muhammad Ali then chimed in, saying, "He who is not courageous enough to take risks will accomplish nothing in life." They then took turns saying, "Fear is a teacher, and I don't skip class."

From this perspective, fear is an emotion that can guide us to become better. However, most of us still view fear as a negative emotion—something we need to eliminate to be in the best state to overcome any problem. This is also true for me. I think that in a *flow* state, people feel tremendous excitement because their skills perfectly match the challenges they face, leaving no room for fear.

So the question is: do top-performing athletes feel no fear when they are about to compete on the world's biggest stage? I once came across a video showing that even elite athletes fear they might not perform well, despite having reached the pinnacle of their sport. However, they use this feeling to their advantage by acknowledging it and pushing through it.

Does taking a deep breath make this feeling disappear? No, but moving forward despite it does. How many times have you been scared of something, gone through it, and afterward wondered why you were so afraid? I think many of us have experienced this. The following passage from *The Miracle Morning* reflects this experience perfectly:

> As individuals, each of us struggled with our own fears and self-doubt. But the moment we crossed that finish line, we had given ourselves the gift of freedom from our fears, our self-doubt, and our self-imposed limitations.
>
> It was in that moment I realized that this is a gift of freedom not reserved for the chosen few, but one that is available to each and every one of us the moment we make the choice to take on challenges that are out of our comfort zone, forcing us to grow, to expand our capacity, to be and do more than we have been and done in the past. This is true freedom.
>
> - Hal Elrod

Thus, I want to suggest that fear is something you need in order to become a better version of yourself and create the life you want. Embrace it and grow.

### Instagram Reel Description

> "The true weight of a man is the gap between who he is and who he knows he should be."
>
> - Marcus Aurelius

The heaviest burden a person carries is rarely visible. It isn't poverty, failure, or even pain itself, but **the silent distance between who one is and who one knows they ought to be**. That gap follows a person everywhere—into moments of rest, success, and even distraction. It shows up as guilt without a clear crime and restlessness without a clear cause.

**Knowing better is both a gift and a curse**. Awareness sharpens conscience, but it also removes excuses. Once a person understands their own potential, every compromise feels deliberate. The mind keeps a quiet record: choices not taken, discipline postponed, courage delayed. Over time, this record becomes heavy.

Many try to escape this weight by lowering their standards, convincing themselves that expectations were unrealistic to begin with. Others drown it in noise—endless entertainment, ambition without meaning, or constant comparison. These methods work temporarily, but the gap never disappears. It waits.

**What makes the burden particularly human is that it isn't imposed from outside**. No authority forces it upon us. **It is self-generated**, born from the knowledge that we are capable of more honesty, more effort, more integrity than we currently show. **That knowledge refuses to be silenced.**

**Yet within this tension lies possibility. The gap is painful, but it also points forward. It reveals direction.** **Growth does not begin with perfection; it begins with refusal to ignore the distance. When a person chooses to close that gap—slowly, imperfectly, but sincerely—the weight changes. It no longer crushes. It guides.**

**Perhaps maturity is learning to carry this burden without denial, and using it not as a source of shame, but as a compass toward becoming whole.**

### The Miracle Morning Description

Human beings are conditioned to have what I refer to as gap focus. We tend to focus on the gaps between where we are in life and where we want to be, the gaps between what we've accomplished and what we want to accomplish, and any perceived gaps between who we are and our idealistic vision of the person we believe we should be.

The problem with this is that constant gap focus can be detrimental to our confidence and self-image, causing us to feel like we don't have enough, haven't accomplished enough, and aren't good enough or, at least, not as good as we should be.

High achievers are typically the worst at this, constantly overlooking or minimizing their accomplishments, beating themselves up over every mistake and imperfection, and never feeling like anything they do is quite good enough!

The irony is that gap focus is a big part of the reason that high achievers are high achievers. Their insatiable desire to close the gap is what fuels their pursuit of excellence and constantly drives them to achieve. Gap focus can be healthy and productive if it comes from a positive, proactive "I'm committed to and excited about fulfilling my potential" perspective, without any feelings of lack. Unfortunately, it rarely does. The average person, even the average high achiever, tends to focus negatively on their gaps.

The highest achievers—those who are balanced and focused on achieving level 10 success in nearly every area of their lives—are exceedingly grateful for what they have, regularly acknowledge themselves for what they've accomplished, and are always at peace with where they are in their lives. It's a dueling idea: "I am doing the best that I can at this moment, and at the same time, I can and will do better." This balanced self-assessment prevents that feeling of lack—of not being, having, doing enough—while still allowing them to constantly strive to close their potential gap in each area.

Typically, when a day, week, month, or year ends and we're in gap-focus mode, it's almost impossible to maintain an accurate assessment of ourselves and our progress. For example, if you had 10 things on your to-do list for the day—even if you completed six of them—your gap focus causes you to feel that you didn't get everything done that you wanted to get done.  
Most people do dozens, even hundreds, of things correctly and a few things wrong during the day. Guess which things people remember and replay in their minds over and over again? Doesn't it make more sense to focus on the hundreds of things you did right? Surely, it's more enjoyable.
