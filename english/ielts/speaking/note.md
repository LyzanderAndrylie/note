# Tips

## Task 1

### Useful information

- **Time:** 4-5 minutes
- You will be asked questions about you and your life.
- **Common topics:**
  - Your home
  - Your family
  - Your work or study
  - Your home town
  - Your likes or dislikes
  - Your interests
- You will only be assessed on your English language skills, not on the content of what you actually say.

> On the other hand, do not ramble on and on. They have around **12 questions** to ask you in **4-5 minutes**. If you spend 2 of those minutes answering a simple question on, for example, where you live, there will not be time for all the questions and you will miss out on some of the marks.

> ⚠️ IELTS Speaking Part 1 answers should be **2-3 sentences long**.

- Extend:
  - Explanation
    - combined details
    - examples
    - Example words: because, and, for example
  - frequency
    - Always
    - Usually
    - Normally
    - Generally
    - Often
    - Frequently
    - Sometimes
    - Occasionally
    - Seldom
    - Rarely
    - Never
    - Hardly ever
  - Contrast:
    - But
    - Although
    - However
    - even though
    - despite
    - while.
  - past comparisons
  - future
    - plan to
- Example:
  - ==Yes, I've been abroad== a couple of times.
  - ==During the week,== I generally go out with friends in the evenings after work and on Saturdays. I go shopping with my best friend. Sundays, I save for seeing my family.
  - I used to socialize with my friend every day, but I don't see them often now ==that I== have a baby and can go out in the evening so much. We probably meet up just a few times a month now.
  - Currently, ==I hardly see== my friends at all, as I spend all my spare time studying for my IELTS exam. When I pass the exam, I will meet up with my mates most evenings like I used to do and spend weekends with them as well.
  - I began studying English at school ==when I was just== six years old, and I have been working on my English skills ever since. I am 23 now, so I've been an English student for 17 years.
  - I cook ==most days of the week.==
  - ==I've always been a fan of== the daily routine called "Savers" by Hal Elrod, which helps me being more intentional ==and hit the ground running== in the morning through reading, writing, and exercising.
  - Well, that's an interesting question. ==I haven't given it much thought before,== but I think it would be great ==to find a balance between the two.==
  - ==Both friends and family are very important to me. But I would say that family holds a slightly more crucial role in my life. Family members are near and dear to me, and I know I can rely on them== ==through thick and thin. That being said,== friends are like a second family and offer ==companionship and== ==support in different ways. My close friends provide a shoulder to cry on== and are always there to support each other. ==Both relationships are vital and enrich my life in unique ways.==
  - I have a relatively small circle of friends. ==I believe in having== a few close friends rather than a large number of acquaintances. This way, I can maintain deeper, more meaningful relationships. My friends and ==I get on well and have a lot in common,== which makes our time together enjoyable. Having a small group allows me to ==give each friendship the attention it deserves and to keep in touch== more easily.
  - My best friend at school was someone I met in first grade. ==We hit it off immediately and became== ==inseparable. We were well matched in terms of interests and personalities. We got on like a house on== ==fire.== Even though we sometimes have our ups and downs, we always manage to work things out and stay close friends.
  - ==Make the most of our time together.==

## Task 2

### Useful information

- **Time:** 3-4 minutes
- The examiner will give you a cue card with a topic written on it.
- It will also have 3-5 bullet points telling you what to include in your talk.
- You will be given **1 minute** for preparation during which you are allowed to make notes.
- You will be asked to speak on this topic for **up to 2 minutes**.
- The examiner will probably ask you one or two questions about your topic.

> 🎯 Do not waste your energy stressing about this, worrying that it will be a subject you know nothing about. It will be what it will be, but you can be sure that it will be related to you in some way, for example:
>
> - something about you
> - something that has happened to you
> - something you have experienced

### Mistakes to Avoid

- ~~Do not speak for the full 2 minutes~~
- ~~Run out of things to say~~
- ~~Have an unstructured talk~~
- ~~Read their notes rather than speak naturally~~
- ~~Panic because they think they know nothing about the topic~~

### Cue Card Guide

2. The examiner will give you a cue card.

> **Example Cue card:**
> Describe something healthy you enjoy doing.
>
> You should say:
>
> - what you do
> - where you do it
> - who you do it with
>
> and explain why you think doing this is healthy.

The most important words on the cue card are the first sentence. This is your _topic_. This is what you must talk about for your 2 minutes. The bullet points are there as a guide as to what you could include in your talk.

> 🎯 The key is to stay focussed on the topic. Use the bullet points as a _guide_ but do not limit yourself to them. You certainly do not have to use all of them and you will not lose marks if you miss some of them out.

- The bullet points are there as a guide
- You do not have to use them all
- You can talk about other things as well
- The key is to stay focussed on the topic

### Structure of Thoughts

|                   |                |                              |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Your thoughts** | • Background   | • Present situation          |
| **Your opinion**  | • Descriptions | • Future situation           |
| **Your feelings** | • Examples     | • Anything else relevant ... |
