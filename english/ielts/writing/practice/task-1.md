### The percentage of children in four kinds of activities, 2009

You should spend about 20 minutes on this task.

The chart below shows the percentage of children in a European country who took part in four kinds of activities in 2009.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

You should write at least 150 words.

#### First attempt (5/9)

The figure depicts the proportion of four activity types of European kids in 2009. Overall, the children across ages, ranging from 5 to 15 years old, participate mostly on watching TV, with average approximately at 80%. On the other side, they rarely took part at playing video games with lower mean at around 50%.

For each age group, they are interested in different types of actions. Starting from the younger (5-8 years old), a significant number of them watch TV. This is also true for 9-11 years old group. However, the teen group with 12-15 years old, they ride bikes for most of them.

Looking at different action type, each age group have a different participation. Watching TV have the highest participation among the group, followed by riding bikes and reading comics. Each of them have a similar percentage of participation. On the other side, specifically for playing video games, the teen group seems to have a lower participation than the younger groups with 30% less.

##### Note

- Proportions: We use the plural form because the chart displays multiple, distinct percentages.
- Age demographics: "Demographics" is plural here because it refers to the three distinct age categories shown in the chart (5-8, 9-11, and 12-15).
- 5-8 year olds (noun):
  - When referring to the children themselves as subjects or objects in a sentence, "year olds" functions as a plural noun.
    - The 5-8 year olds cycle often. (Noun)
  - When describing the age of a noun, "years old" functions as an adjective phrase.
    - The children are 5-8 years old. (Adjective)
  - Exception:
    - when you put the age directly in front of a noun (like "teenager") to describe it, it becomes a compound adjective.
    - In English, compound adjectives are always singular, so we drop the "s" and connect the words with hyphens
    - The 15-year-old teenager plays video games because he is 15 years old.
- Participate in …; take part in …

#### Example

The provided bar chart illustrates the proportions of children in a specific European country who participated in four different activities: watching TV, reading commics, playing video games, and riding bikes in the year 2009. The data is categorized by three age groups: 5-8, 9-11, and 12-15 years old.

Overall, watching television was the most popular activity across all age demographics, whereas playing video games ==was generally the least favoured.== Additionally, while participation in watching TV and playing video games decreased with age, the popularity of riding bikes increased.

Looking at the sedentary activities, watching TV ==saw the highest engagement, starting at== nearly 95% for 5-8 ==year olds before dropping to== 80% for both the 9-11 and 12-15 age brackets. Reading comics remained relatively stable across all ages. In contrast, playing video games was most popular amog the youngest children at approximately 60%, but this figure fell significantly to just 40% for the 12-15 year olds.

Regarding physical activity, riding bikes ==showed an upward trend with age.== While roughly 65% of 5-8 year olds and 60% of 9-11 year olds cycled, this activity ==saw a sharp increase== in popularity among teenagers, ==peaking at== over 80% for the 12-15 age group.

#### Example

- Introduction:
  - The bar graph illustrates how many male and female students were studying full-time and part-time in Britain during the years 1970/1, 1980/1 and 1990/1.
- Paragraph 2
  - Overall, the number of students in full-time education ==increased over this time.== ==With regard to== part-time education, there was a ==steady increase in women choosing== to study part-time but for men, the level fluctuated.
- Paragraph 3
  - Between the academic periods 1970/1 and 1990/1, ==the figures== for men studying full-time ==grew in steady increments, beginning at== 100,000 in 1970/1 ==and rising== to nearly 300,000 in 1990/1. For women, the numbers had also risen to around 300,000 by 1990/1 ==on an increasing trend.== Notably, however, ==the figure for females in full-time education in 1970/1 was roughly half that of men,== showing a greater ==take-up== than men between 1970/1 and 1980/1.
- Paragraph 4
  - A different pattern is shown for part-time education. The graph reveals a steady increase in women engaged in part-time study, ==rising from== 750,000 in 1970/1 ==to== 1,000,000 by 1990/1. For men, on the other hand, ==the figure fluctuated.== From 1,000,000 in 1970/1, they ==had dropped== by almost 200,000 by 1980/1 but ==recovered slightly in numbers== by 1990/1. Over the whole time period, the trend between men and women in part-time education reversed, with male students being ==greater in number== in 1970/1 and female students taking ==pole position== by 1990/1.

#### Note

- "In steady increments" (usually plural): In is used to denote the manner, steps, or units of change (e.g., in stages, in chunks, in batches, in small amounts). An increment is a discrete step or chunk of addition, so you move in increments.
- "On an increasing trend": On is used because a trend is conceptualized as a trajectory, line, or course (e.g., on a path, on a trajectory, on an upward track, on the rise).
- “that of” and “those of”
  - **Definition:** "That of" is a demonstrative pronoun phrase used to replace a singular noun (or uncountable noun) and avoid awkward repetition when making comparisons. Its plural counterpart is "those of."

  **Core Rules & Mechanics:**
  1. **Ensuring Parallel Comparisons:**
     A common error is comparing an attribute of something directly to the entire entity itself. "That of" ensures you compare the same category of things.
     - _Faulty:_ The throughput of System A is higher than System B. _(Compares throughput to a system)._
     - _Correct:_ The throughput of System A is higher than that of System B. _("that" = the throughput)._
  2. **Number Agreement (Singular vs. Plural):**
     - Singular / Uncountable $\rightarrow$ **that of**:
       - _"The latency of the new service is lower than that of the legacy service."_
     - Plural $\rightarrow$ **those of**:
       - _"The latency metrics of the new service are lower than those of the legacy service."_

  **Alternatives & When to Omit:**
  While precise and standard in formal/academic writing, overusing "that of" can feel stiff. Common alternatives include:
  - Possessive nouns ('s):
    - _"The performance of Model A exceeds Model B's."_
  - Rephrasing for brevity:
    - _"System A has higher throughput than System B."_
