## First Attempt (6.0/9)

It is argued that all lawbreakers should be put in jail regardless of their crimes, while others support better approaches for the minor lawbreakers through other activities, such as serving people in a local community. This essay fully agrees with the alternatives, as they give an opportunity for the minor lawbreakers to fix and align their behaviour with the law. On the other hand, although the law itself should be enforced to all cases, I think judging them without considering their severity would be a flaw.

People often make mistakes in their life, including actions that break the law. However, those actions differ in the eyes of the law. Some cases are considered fatal, while others can be seen as a minor ignorance of the rules. For the minor ones, from my perspective, they are fixable in the sense that the law enforcers can put them in social activities to improve their moral and social values, such as contributing to other people. This can help them to be a better member of the community. For example, my uncle frequently bypass traffic lights when they are still showing a yellow light. After getting too many fines, an instructor came to him, and he was tasked with educating others about the importance of traffic lights. After a while, he learned that passing through when the light is still yellow can cause a tragedy to himself and his family. After this realization, he changed his mind and never break the rules again.

In contrast, if we blindly consider all crime cases to be the same, an acidental law-breaking action can lead to an imprisonment. This may scare people to take actions to help others, as mishaps can cause them to spend their times in prison. For instance, a very strict law enforcement in China cause their people to be a bit hesitant to aid others, as their actions can be wrongly categorized as breaking the law, resulting in a severe punishment.

In conclusion, while the law should be followed and enforced to all people, considering their impact are also important for the law enforcer. Putting the minor cases into social activities should benefit the society more than putting them directly to jail.

### Note Section: Summary of Issues & Corrections

- **Issue 1 (GRA - Subject-Verb Agreement)**:
  - _Incorrect_: _"a very strict law enforcement in China cause their people..."_
  - _Correction_: _"strict law enforcement in China **causes**..."_
  - _Reason_: The subject noun is singular (_law enforcement_), which requires a singular verb (_causes_).
- **Issue 2 (GRA - Gerund Subject Agreement)**:
  - _Incorrect_: _"considering their impact **are** also important..."_
  - _Correction_: _"considering their impact **is** also essential..."_
  - _Reason_: A gerund phrase acting as a grammatical subject (_considering..._) is singular and must take the singular verb _is_.
- **Issue 3 (GRA - Tense Inconsistency in Narrative)**:
  - _Incorrect_: _"my uncle frequently **bypass** traffic lights... and never **break** the rules again."_
  - _Correction_: _"my uncle frequently **bypassed**... and never **broke** the rules again."_
  - _Reason_: When narrating a past completed sequence of events, all verbs must consistently remain in the past tense.
- **Issue 4 (GRA - Countability and Noun Agreement)**:
  - _Incorrect_: _"in their life"_, _"spend their times"_, _"an imprisonment"_, _"benefit the society"_.
  - _Correction_: _"in their **lives**"_, _"spend **time**"_, _"**imprisonment**"_, _"benefit **society**"_.
  - _Reason_:
    - Plural possessive _their_ requires plural _lives_.
    - _Time_ (duration) and _imprisonment_ (state of penal detention) are uncountable nouns in these contexts.
    - _Society_ in a general, abstract sense does not take the definite article _the_.
- **Issue 5 (GRA - Preposition Choices)**:
  - _Incorrect_: _"enforced to all cases"_, _"putting them directly to jail"_.
  - _Correction_: _"enforced **in** all cases / applied **to** all cases"_, _"sending them directly **to prison / into jail**"_.
  - _Reason_: Laws are enforced _in_ situations/jurisdictions or applied _to_ people; offenders are sentenced _to prison_ or put _in/into jail_.
- **Issue 6 (LR - Imprecise Word Choice & Collocations)**:
  - _Incorrect_: _"minor ignorance of the rules"_, _"fatal cases"_, _"put them in social activities"_, _"changed his mind"_.
  - _Correction_: _"**minor infractions / petty misdemeanors**"_, _"**heinous / violent crimes**"_, _"**assign them to community service**"_, _"**reformed his conduct**"_.
  - _Reason_:
    - _Ignorance_ means lack of knowledge, not an act of violation.
    - _Fatal_ strictly denotes death-causing incidents, whereas the author intended serious felonies.
    - _Social activities_ suggests recreational gatherings; the accurate legal term is _community service_ or _restorative justice_.
    - _Changed his mind_ describes an ordinary shift in opinion, not moral/behavioral rehabilitation.
- **Issue 7 (TR / CC - Academic Tone vs. Personal Anecdote)**:
  - _Observation_: Dedicating half of a body paragraph to a personal story about an uncle reduces academic formality and analytical breadth.
  - _Correction_: ==Replace personal narratives with objective sociological and institutional examples== (e.g., driver rehabilitation courses, community restitution orders).
- **Issue 8 (CC - Misused Discourse Markers)**:
  - _Incorrect_: Using _"On the other hand"_ in the introduction after already giving the thesis.
  - _Correction_: Reserve _"On the other hand"_ strictly for introducing a contrasting perspective when balanced with _"On the one hand"_.

In conclusion, subjecting all lawbreakers to prison terms is an ==overly punitive and economically inefficient policy==.
